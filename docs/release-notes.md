# What’s new in RMT Compose v2

You can tune a note to a resolution that has yet to sound. In bar 5 of the opening canon, the green bass takes its A from the following E in the red theme. Change that E and listen to the A move with it. The new score, editor and playback controls let you hear the consequence of that choice, carry it into another piece and export the tuning to MIDI.

The changes below are ordered for composers, comparing the published `main` revision `7303649` with `v2` at `c21f71e` and the End-drop default prepared for this release.

1. **Compose toward a later note.** Pitch, start time and duration can follow different notes. v2 orders evaluation and checks cycles per property, so two notes can depend on one another in different ways. A bass can take its pitch from a future resolution while its entrance still follows the previous note. Pitch-only references and timed key changes give a passage an unsounded harmonic anchor. [Trace the A and E in bar 5](/tutorials/workflows/canon-and-cadences) · [Dependencies](/user-guide/notes/dependencies)
2. **Hear the relationships in a rewritten canon and eight revoiced progressions.** The startup canon now draws both basses from one line, with one deliberately different tuning in bar 5. The library has 81 modules, including the full canon and two parts under **Custom**. All eight progressions and cadences have new octave placements around their harmonic roots. In **V7 – I**, the dominant root resolves to the tonic while the lowest sounding voice descends a whole tone and another voice stays put. [Canon and cadences](/tutorials/workflows/canon-and-cadences) · [Complete library](/user-guide/modules/module-library)
3. **Write exact tempered intervals alongside pure ratios.** A fifth can be `3/2` or `2^(7/12)`. Both relations can remain exact. Canvas pitch labels show the evaluated relation to the BaseNote, including symbolic powers where representable. A leading `~` requests an approximate number. **Play tempered (12-TET)** lets you hear a keyboard-tuned interpretation and switch back without rewriting a pitch. [Expression syntax](/reference/expressions/syntax) · [Pure and tempered playback](/user-guide/playback/audio#pure-tempered)
4. **Shape a phrase with dynamics and envelopes.** Set relative amplitude, attack and release on individual notes or inherit them. Note height shows amplitude. An optional envelope inside the note shows loudness over time, and a drawn tail shows what rings past the written end. The widget identifies an explicit value, an inherited value or the instrument’s own envelope. [Amplitude](/reference/properties/amplitude) · [Attack and release](/reference/properties/attack-release)
5. **Play a line as one musician.** Notes inherit their instrument along their start-time relationships, through rests and silences. Pinning an instrument starts another player. The widget identifies the player a note belongs to. Violin notes can join in a slur or start a new bow, and restriking a piano pitch damps its previous sound. [Instruments and players](/user-guide/playback/instruments)
6. **Take the tuning into a DAW or a MIDI player.** **Export MIDI** creates a Type 1 file at 960 ticks per quarter, assigns General MIDI programs and uses independent pitch bends to represent pitches between keys. Amplitude becomes velocity. Fifteen pitched channels are available per port. The export reports when overlapping notes need more ports, or when a pitch cannot be represented. A programmatic MPE export is also available. [MIDI export and receiving-device settings](/user-guide/modules/exporting-midi) · [Exporter API](/developer/api/midi-export)
7. **Set the pulse with one time unit and audition a different tempo live.** The BaseNote duration is the unit in seconds. Measure durations and note entrances can be written in multiples of it. The widget shows the corresponding BPM and each measure’s length in units. The live slider defaults to a 20–300 BPM range, which you can change. Move it while the piece plays, return to its written pace or commit the new tempo. A dropped module uses the receiving score’s unit. [Duration and tempo](/reference/properties/duration) · [Tempo controls](/user-guide/interface/module-bar#live-tempo)
8. **Shift the tuning on every repeat.** The score can store a repeat interval such as `9/8` or `2^(1/6)`. Each loop starts at the resulting pitch, and the BaseNote and widget show the pass you hear. Keep the result within an octave or let it continue rising or falling. The startup canon is ready to try at `9/8`. [Loop playback](/user-guide/playback/transport#rising-or-falling-each-time-round)
9. **Edit related notes and measures together.** Shift-click toggles an item. A Shift-drag can begin over a note, and on touch a hold lets you add by tapping or dragging. Select a measure by its triangle. Move, copy or delete a mixed phrase, or change duration across notes and measures. For notes, select a chain by pitch, time or duration, then set a value, multiply the existing relationship, restore inheritance, apply a colour, choose an instrument for the whole line or start a player on each note. Undo and redo retain the group when its members are present. [Selection and group editing](/user-guide/notes/selection) · [Touch gestures](/user-guide/interface/mobile)
10. **Build a phrase by joining endings.** Dropping a module on a note now defaults to its **End**. Choose **Start** for a simultaneous entrance. An existing saved choice is restored. Dragging a note’s entrance exactly onto another note’s or silence’s end can write `[P].t + [P].d`. Change the earlier duration and the joined note comes with it. New measures likewise carry their own duration and can follow a preceding measure’s end. [Module bar](/user-guide/interface/module-bar) · [Editing notes](/user-guide/notes/editing-notes)
11. **Hear new and revised instruments.** Two harmonium voices make beating and pure intervals easy to hear. The piano uses longer, retuned recordings and register-dependent decay. The violin sustains its looped samples for the written length. Sample zones use measured pitch corrections. Square and sawtooth use single oscillators, and the vibraphone’s modes decay individually. [Instrument guide](/user-guide/playback/instruments) · [Instrument implementation](/developer/audio/instruments)
12. **Move around a playing score with cleaner transitions.** Pause, stop, restart and seek use audio-clock fades. A click on empty canvas parks at the time you chose for the next Play. Written note-offs can ring through their releases. Dense chords are scheduled in smaller batches, the voice control offers budgets of 128 to 4,096 (default 256) and prioritises fading tails for removal and the output limiter catches peaks near the device. [Transport](/user-guide/playback/transport) · [Scheduling and measured output](/developer/audio/streaming)
13. **Work with larger scores and keep useful undo history.** The Load Module ceiling rises from 10,000 entries and 3 MiB to **500,000 entries and 120 MiB**. IDs can reach 16,777,215. Autosave migrates the previous browser score into IndexedDB and defers writes past the edit frame. History shares unchanged pages so small edits to a long score can retain earlier states. The release check restored the expected score for all 20 undos and 20 redos on the 500,000-note fixture. [Saving and history](/user-guide/modules/saving-modules) · [Limits and measurement conditions](/developer/performance)
14. **Keep a dense overview responsive and a selected note readable.** Drawing, picking, labels and relation highlights now concentrate work on visible or changed notes. **Reduce detail above** defaults to 1,536 visible notes. Choose another threshold or **Never** under Appearance. Colour fields accept CSS colour forms, numeric controls have their own step arrows and the note-creation area is easier to distinguish in the widget. [Appearance settings](/user-guide/interface/settings#appearance) · [Rendering](/developer/architecture/rendering)
15. **Use one expression language from the first edit to the saved score.** The editor and shipped library now use the compact DSL throughout. Recognised legacy file expressions, timing fields and instrument assignments are converted when loaded. Save a new copy to keep the converted version. The evaluator is now JavaScript throughout. The development tools include reproducible history, scheduling, output, gesture and rendering checks. [Compatibility and file format](/reference/module-schema) · [Developer guide](/developer/)

For a first encounter with v2, play the canon, lengthen the opening bass note and hear the next entrance move. The tutorial includes saving the new version and undoing the change. [Make your first change](/getting-started/first-composition)
